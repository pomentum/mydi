# TST-0002: Follow a drum machine

**Target:** a drum machine's clock followed and sent on to a synth, every pulse counted, its tempo and bars told.
**Status:** Covered

## The use
In a small set-up the drum machine is the master. A program follows its clock, sends it on to other devices that need one, and plays
its own parts in time, knowing where the bar is and when the master stops, moves or is lost.

## How it is shown
- The follower on generated sources: steady, jittered, ramping, jumping, dropping out (`follow_test.go`, `ramp_test.go`,
  `remake_test.go`) [V-exp: the suite, in CI].
- Recorded drum-machine clocks replayed: Starts whose first pulse comes milliseconds after the last, and deliveries the system held
  (`restart_test.go`, `held_delivery_test.go`, `tests/testdata/`) [V-exp].
- On hardware: a synth as master followed into a second device for 53 bars, 5,233 pulses heard and 5,233 sent, the phase 0.63 ms median
  ([the follow report](../reports/following-a-midi-clock.md)) [V-exp]. A pass: the count equal from Start to Stop, every pulse within
  half an interval of the source's, most within a quarter ([ADR-0005](../adrs/0005-a-followed-clock-that-moves.md) part C).
- An echo of the hub's own clock never locked on (`echo_test.go`, `echo_loop_test.go`).

## Limits
Linux's raw MIDI gives no stamp of its own, so the follower fits on the reader's wake-up there; Ableton Link and MIDI timecode are not
followed ([ADR-0004](../adrs/0004-following-a-devices-clock.md) part F).
